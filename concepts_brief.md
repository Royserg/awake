# Awake: cinematic pixel-eye intro and compact update wait

* **Author:** Awake project with Codex
* **Date:** 2026-10-26

## Overview

The main risk is letting a cosmetic intro control startup or update work. Awake already activates in native setup; the current splash instead stays visible according to updater timing. A longer animation alone would not produce the requested bounded intro.

Preserve the recognizable existing pixel eye, open silhouette, crisp pixels, and monochrome appearance. Present the short opening in a borderless overlay spanning the selected screen, with the eye centered over a dimmed backdrop and soft radial gradient. Give the opening an eased rhythm, a short hold, and one restrained pixelated wave travelling outward toward the screen edges. The eye is not stretched to fill the larger backdrop. On every ordinary app launch, finish disappearance and hide the window within 1.5–2 seconds of the visible intro starting under normal foreground playback. Activation must not wait for the intro or update work. Confirmed updates are an explicit timing exception: keep the eye closed with a subtle waiting idle in a compact window during download and installation, retain automatic restart, and play the screen-sized opening on the restarted launch. The compact waiting window must not keep the desktop covered or dimmed.

The user approved proceeding to design, confirmed animation on every app launch, and requested a replay preview for tuning before visual finalization. The compact update-waiting exception and screen-sized intro were confirmed after the Goldfish review; those expanded requirements were not part of the concept those six reviewers evaluated. Naturalness and subtlety still require an actual-size animated preview.

## Analysis

All six Goldfish lenses completed in fresh contexts: design, codebase details, zoom-out, comprehension, implementation readiness, and structural. They ran with bounded concurrency of at most two reviewers, not all six at once. Each returned an actual completion and explicitly confirmed reading its persona and every selected source: `src/routes/+page.svelte`, `src/routes/eye-splashscreen.ts`, `src/routes/README.md`, `src-tauri/src/lib.rs`, `src-tauri/tauri.conf.json`, and `package.json`. No missing reads were reported. Reviewers were read-only and received no other reviewer's findings. These source reviews establish implementation constraints, not visual acceptance or runtime test results.

**🟡 Major — Distinguish ordinary startup from an update wait.** Today, `+page.svelte:21–40` awaits the update flow and then adds a 1000 ms delay on no-update/error paths. Ordinary launches need their own bounded intro without that extra post-check wait. The later user decision explicitly permits the splash to stay visible during a confirmed download and installation: keep the eye closed with a restrained waiting idle in a compact window, preserve `check → downloadAndInstall → relaunch`, and open in the screen-sized overlay on the restarted launch. Desktop dimming ends with the short intro and is absent during the update wait. The technical design must resolve slow checks, late update discovery, and update/restart failure so none leaves an unexplained waiting eye. Do not move the updater to another subsystem solely for this visual change.

Native activation already starts NoSleep in `AppState::new()` during setup (`lib.rs:108–118,331–333`). Preserve that ownership and ordering. Do not activate only after the eye opens, and do not interpret the animation as proof of successful activation. The duration describes visible presentation, not a promise that process launch and WebView initialization take at most two seconds.

**🟡 Major — Separate eye geometry from the screen-sized backdrop.** The current window is 300×300 (`tauri.conf.json:17–20`), with a nominal 240×240 eye area and 15 CSS-pixel cells (`+page.svelte:56–59`; `eye-splashscreen.ts:264–279,667–672`). The user has now approved a screen-sized, borderless intro centered over desktop dimming and a soft radial gradient. Keep the eye recognizable and pixel-crisp; do not scale its canvas to fill the screen. A screen-wide wave and dimming layer need independent geometry. The current opaque black background and native window configuration do not implement desktop transparency. Display selection, focus, stacking, dismissal, and platform support require technical design. Preserve compact presentation for update waiting and the existing timer window.

**🟡 Major — Easing needs behavior, not a label.** The current renderer special-cases `hold` but otherwise interpolates numerically in a straight line (`eye-splashscreen.ts:503–516,569–613`). Changing an easing string alone will do nothing. Prefer a small explicit mapping from elapsed time to opening progress, or timed grid-aligned poses using the existing renderer. Preserve the canonical open-eye pixels separately from timing; avoid squashing, stretching, rotating, blurring, or scaling the eye. Pixel-aligned reveal and eased timing are candidates, not evidence of natural eyelid motion.

**🟡 Major — A wave can compete with the eye.** Start with one sparse stepped contour, without repeated pulses, glow, particles, or a flash. Keep cell size fixed while the contour travels outward. A dense effect would add visual noise and allocation work: the renderer currently clears and rebuilds SVG layer contents each frame (`eye-splashscreen.ts:639–674`). Prefer a sparse fixed or precomputed wave and verify its cost at the selected screen resolution; do not fill a screen-sized area with per-frame pixel nodes. The lighter comparison is the same opening with the wave temporarily disabled; omitting the wave from the final feature would change the agreed concept and needs user review.

**🟡 Major — Completion must survive visual failure.** Use one local timeline owner and one idempotent dismissal path. Schedule an ordinary-intro fallback independently of animation-frame completion, with the confirmed download/install waiting state explicitly exempt from that short deadline. Start updater work even if visual setup throws. If rendering is interrupted, use elapsed time when it resumes rather than replaying overdue motion. Reduced motion should omit eyelid travel and the travelling wave, show the unchanged open eye briefly, and dismiss promptly; this is a proposed exception to the normal 1.5–2 second sequence.

The page currently registers no animation teardown, although `destroy()` already cancels playback and removes its SVG (`+page.svelte:13–36`; `eye-splashscreen.ts:311–316,356–359`). Stop ordinary intro work at completion; stop the waiting idle when update work ends or the app restarts. Clean up animation frames, timers, motion listeners, and preview handlers on teardown. Do not cancel installation merely because the intro finishes. Log local failures concisely; native hiding currently ignores errors (`lib.rs:12–18`), so a failed hide must not be reported as successful dismissal. No cloud telemetry or tracing system is warranted.

**🟢 Nit — Preview and launch policy are separate.** Debug “Show Splash” only shows and focuses the existing window (`lib.rs:423–428`); it does not replay the animation. The user confirmed a replay preview and animation on every app launch. Preview must replay the normal opening/wave and simulate the closed-eye update wait and restart without real update checks, installation, relaunch, or activation changes. No install-only flag or persistent launch counter is needed.

## Goals vs. Non-Goals

* **In-Scope Goals:** Preserve the existing eye's recognizable open pose, crisp pixel geometry, and monochrome identity, centered within a screen-sized cinematic intro. Improve opening cadence; add one secondary outward pixel wave during opening; hold briefly; disappear and hide within the ordinary-launch duration. During confirmed downloads and installation, show a closed-eye idle in a compact window without desktop dimming until automatic restart, then use the screen-sized opening on the new launch. Provide the requested replay preview. Keep activation and tray/timer interaction independent. Handle reduced motion, visual failure, and cleanup.
* **Out-of-Scope Non-Goals:** A redesigned eye, changed tray icons, a new animation framework or dependency, a generic effects engine, backend startup orchestration, cloud services or telemetry, an updater redesign, install-only flags, launch counters, or new persistent settings.

## Key Decisions & Staging for Next Steps

1. **Use a small presentation change.** Keep existing native activation, update contracts, and the compact timer window. Design the newly approved screen-sized intro separately from the compact update wait; native presentation configuration will need review. Reuse the renderer and hide command, preserving documented playback/callback contracts if touched. Keep ordinary presentation completion separate from updater ownership, with a distinct closed-eye waiting state during confirmed updates. Update the route README's timing and lifecycle description when implementation changes it. No data migration is proposed; rollback should retain existing IPC and updater compatibility.

2. **Preview before choosing motion details.** A starting schedule is below; these are audition values, not settled visual decisions. The wave shares the total budget and reaches the agreed boundary while opening is still underway.

   | Time from visible start | Proposed presentation |
   | --- | --- |
   | 0–850 ms | Eased, pixel-aligned opening to the existing open pose |
   | About 200–850 ms, overlapping opening | One restrained outward wave reaches the window boundary |
   | 850–1450 ms | Short open-eye hold |
   | 1450–1750 ms | Brief disappearance, then native window hiding |

   The current surface is black and opaque (`+page.svelte:64`); desktop transparency is not configured. The new screen-sized overlay must define how desktop dimming and the radial gradient are composed and removed. Preview the complete desktop handoff; fading only eye content would leave the backdrop behind.

3. **Keep visual choices open for user review.** Settle the opening curve and hold length; wave contour, density/brightness and onset; whether reaching screen “edges” includes corners; eye scale, radial backdrop strength, display selection, and the disappearance treatment. Opacity creates gray composites, so clarify strict black/white versus grayscale fading before choosing it. Launch frequency is confirmed as every app launch. Review the reduced-motion treatment for both the ordinary intro and update wait. None of these choices is established by source inspection.

4. **Accept only against concrete evidence.** Compare the eye at the real target-screen size and compact update-wait size, including Retina scaling and multiple-display arrangements. Preview the complete ordinary sequence and record foreground visibility ending in 1500–2000 ms when no download/install is active. Separately verify the compact update wait: closed eye and subtle idle throughout download/install, no screen-sized dimming layer, automatic restart, then the screen-sized opening. Verify slow/offline checks, late update discovery, download/install/relaunch errors, animation setup failure, interrupted playback, reduced motion, native hide failure, teardown, and isolated preview replay. Confirm tray/timer use remains available and no visual frame loop continues after completion. Run the existing `pnpm check` and `pnpm build` during implementation; neither command nor runtime preview was run for this brief.

5. **Use the objections to limit scope.** If the wave dominates or the opening reads mechanically, first reduce contour density and retune timing within the same budget. Compare with opening-only playback to judge its value, then return the visual decision to the user. Do not solve a local splash problem by expanding the application's infrastructure.
